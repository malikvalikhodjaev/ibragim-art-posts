The sculpture's surroundings are constantly changing. Clouds pass behind its silhouette, a reflection appears below, ripples break up the colour, and the evening light alters the relationship between figure and background. A single object appears in multiple states. Water and shore play a role in the perception of the work as tangibly as distance and the chosen vantage point.

In 2024, Ibragim Valikhodjaev participated as a sculptor in an international floating sculpture project in Hulan, China. Videos on his channel show him working with metal forms and a river exhibit during the day and evening. This is a collaborative project by various artists; Valikhodjaev is both a participant and the author of his work. This context allows us to explore both his professional practice and the possibilities of open-air sculpture.

His work at Hulan demonstrates Valikhodjaev's curiosity about new materials and modes of artistic expression. In his painting, his exploration centres on colour, plane, and composition; in sculpture, these questions are complemented by volume, silhouette, light, and real space. The floating object presents a unique challenge: the image changes with perspective, light, and reflection, and the surrounding environment shapes how we perceive the sculpture.

## From sheet metal to a distinctive silhouette

In the first video above, showing the work process, the artist is working with a red metal form with shaped outlines. At this stage, the role of contour is clearly visible. In a flat sculpture, the outer edge becomes one of the primary means of expression: it conveys direction, movement, and the relationship of the pieces. Even from a distance, a clear silhouette retains its recognisability.

Sculptural form becomes expressive through its curves, volumes and transitions. In a work made from metal surfaces, the sculptural form is constructed through the contours, rotations, and mutual arrangement of the elements. The material retains its distinctive character, and the artistic image emerges from how the artist organises it in space.

A gap within the form also plays a role in the composition. Through it, the sky, water, or distant shore are visible. This negative space—a shaped opening—is perceived together with the surrounding material. On a river, it is especially noticeable, as its background changes throughout the day.

## The riverbank and the distant view

The river exhibition is designed for a wider view than a small exhibition hall. Viewers can stand on the bank, view the work from an angle, and examine several objects at once. In these settings, overall proportions and a coherent silhouette are of great importance. Small details become apparent later, upon closer inspection or in a close-up video.

The vibrant colouring separates the metal form from its natural surroundings. Red can stand out clearly against greenery, a bright sky, or cold water. Its impact, however, depends on the lighting: direct sunlight highlights the surface, while overcast weather softens the contrasts. The work enters a changing colour environment beyond the artist’s control.

The distant vantage point provides, above all, an image of the whole. A side view reveals the thickness of the planes, the connections, and the spatial rotations. Therefore, it's important to view the sculpture from several angles: a frontal shot reveals the silhouette, while camera movement helps us understand the structure of the form itself and its connection to the river space.

## Reflection as a continuation of the image

In calm water, the sculpture takes on a reflected silhouette. This can be perceived as an extension of the vertical form, visually enlarging it. When the surface begins to move, the image breaks into stripes and colour reflections. The encounter between the rigid metallic outline and the shifting reflection creates an additional expressiveness unique to aquatic environments.

The line of contact with the water becomes a crucial boundary. Above it is an object with a stable form; below it is its constantly changing image. At some angles, the eye combines these parts into a single composition; at others, the object and its reflection are clearly distinguished. Video footage conveys this change better than a single still image.

Evening shots reveal a new relationship between light and shadow. Details of the shore recede into the darkness, highlighting illuminated elements and highlights. The sculpture can be perceived more comprehensively, through several accents of light. The changing time of day reveals qualities of the form that were not prominent in daylight.

This project allows us to follow the artist’s exploration beyond the canvas. The relationship between form and emptiness, stable contour and moving water, each time produces a different visual result. Valikhodjaev participated in Hulan as a sculptor and the author of his own work; the international exhibition brought together independent works by various artists. His contribution reveals one aspect of a practice open to new sculptural challenges.

## Artist in an international project

Participation in Hulan reveals the sculptural side of Valikhodjaev's practice. For the painter, colour typically exists on the surface of the canvas; here, it interacts with metallic material, real space, and natural light. This allows him to juxtapose his interest in the spot of colour with the need to create a convincing spatial form.

In the international river exhibition, each work retains its own distinctive authorship, while the overall landscape unites diverse approaches. Some forms may work through silhouette, others through volume or alternating spaces. The juxtaposition helps to appreciate the diversity of sculptural language and the significance of the specific location for each object.

The first video above, showing the work process, should be viewed in conjunction with the general views of the exhibition. First, the journey from material to form becomes clear; then, the work emerges as it exists on water. Thus, the project unfolds in two interconnected dimensions: as the sculptor's labor and as a work of art addressed to the viewer in an open space.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
