First, you notice the orange. It fills the spaces between the dark, rounded fruits, runs in wide stripes, curves, and rises toward the right edge. Then the characteristic pomegranate crowns, reddish sides, and individual ochre strokes appear. A recognisable object unfolds within a powerful colour scheme.

In Ibragim Valikhodjaev's "Pomegranates," the still life offers an opportunity to explore artistic thinking within a small range of motifs. Everything is there for a careful discussion of painting: the relationships of mass, the change in repeating form, the direction of the brushwork, and the thick, visible brushstrokes. It's helpful to first view the painting as a whole, then return to a few fruits within a close-up detail.

## Orange makes the first impression

Wide areas of orange surround the almost black fruits. The difference in lightness makes their contours particularly noticeable; the richness of the warm field creates a sense of inner energy. Enough colour remains between the fruits for the surrounding surface to act as an independent part of the composition.

At the top, orange meets a light greyish area and a large dark plane. These more subdued masses alter the intensity of the adjacent colour. The colour scheme is based on specific juxtapositions: the same orange is perceived differently next to a light background, dark fruit, and a reddish brushstroke.

## Each pomegranate is a little different

The fruits are similar in overall roundness, but differ in the tilt, size, and position of the crown. Some are shown almost straight, others are rotated; some approach the edge and partially extend beyond the image. These variations prevent the group from becoming a uniform row.

Dark silhouettes bring the still life together, while small light strokes refine the subject matter. The crown can be identified by a few directional brushstrokes. The larger, less detailed mass of the fruit remains nearby. The artist distributes the detail so that it works seamlessly with the whole.

## The surface also tells a story

In the close-up detail, furrows and unevenness of the paint layer are visible. The orange is painted in different directions, thickly in places; reddish and greenish hues appear within the dark fruit. What from a distance appeared to be a nearly solid silhouette, up close, reveals a complex painted surface.

The direction of the brushstroke helps us sense the rotation of the rounded object and the movement of the plane beneath it. The border remains quite clear, yet within it, a multitude of changes exist. The detail shows how the overall shape is maintained without a smooth, uniform filling.

## The table becomes a system of directions

Orange stripes and angular borders create a slanted movement, drawing the eye between the fruits and returning to the adjacent silhouette. Space is constructed through planes of colour; the familiar surface acquires a more intense, focused character.

The still life is easily recognisable, but its structure offers a second level of reading. If you momentarily look past the names of the objects, you'll see rounded dark masses, wide warm spaces, and small light accents. The perception of the subject and composition complement each other.

## One Subject within a Wider Exploration

Valikhodjaev's interest in colour and form permeates various themes: the people and everyday life of the Namangan series, the exploration of red in RED, and the abstraction of New View. "Pomegranates" allows one to explore a similar range of questions in still life: what holds attention, how to connect repetitions, and how powerful can a colour field be?

Start with the overall composition, find a single fruit, and trace its edge. After studying a detail, look again at the painting as a whole: the small ochre strokes and twists of form will be more noticeable. A careful return reveals a familiar motif as a sequence of precise painterly decisions.

## What changes at the boundary of an object

The smallest areas here are capable of holding the entire composition together. The meeting of the dark fruit with the orange field creates a clear roundness; the light-coloured crown in another area conveys direction. A narrow, coloured gap remains between the two pomegranates. Its shape connects the objects, but simultaneously separates them. If you trace these gaps, it becomes clear that the still life is constructed both by the position of the fruits and the space between them.

At the lower edge, some of the forms approach the edge of the canvas. This lends a sense of intimacy to the composition: the viewer encounters the objects almost at arm's length. The upper light and dark planes create a different scale and anchor the surroundings. The composition combines an intimate motif with a powerful colour tension. The small format creates an experience akin to the artist's longer canvases: the large masses appear first, and individual brushstrokes become visible only upon return.

Compare "Pomegranates" with the early "Jugs." In both still lifes, the artist selects a limited range of recurring objects. However, the light vessels are revealed through subtle hues, while the dark fruits are revealed through a strong juxtaposition with orange. A single genre allows for different tempos and degrees of contrast. This comparison demonstrates Valikhodjaev's colouristic exploration in motion: the task changes with the setting and the chosen relationship of colour to form.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
