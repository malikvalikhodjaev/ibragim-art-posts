In "Bouquets," several vessels and floral groupings form a unified composition. Comparisons with "Daisies in a Ceramic Vase" and "Still Life with Roses" reveal how Valikhodjaev constructs colour, silhouette, and the space between objects in different ways.

The three works are not reduced to a single decorative motif. Each has its own scale, density, and tempo: from a few vases on a light table to a tall bouquet and a free-flowing scattering of flowers. Let's begin with "Still Life with Roses," where the brushwork and ceramic pattern are particularly easy to observe up close.

## The bouquet goes beyond the strict silhouette

The upper portion of the composition features free, diverging directions. Some flower heads are wide open, while others are compressed into dark knots. The stems intersect and curve; some light petals approach the background. The bouquet's outline remains fluid, with open spaces between individual shapes.

The yellow and orange areas are the first to catch the eye. Nearby, the dark red flowers gain a more solid character, while the white ones reveal themselves through subtle hues and brushstroke directions. The differences give each area its own strength, supporting the overall movement.

## A stroke becomes a petal

A close-up detail allows us to see how form emerges from the application of paint. Several brushstrokes revolve around the floral centre, a light trace crosses a rich field, and a dark area deepens the adjacent colour. The texture preserves the brushstroke's direction.

In this painting, the eye simultaneously notices the flower and the way it is painted. A short stroke can create the edge of a petal; a longer one can connect several parts. The persuasiveness of the image is determined by the relationships between the brushstrokes, distributed across the entire surface of the bouquet.

## The vase sets a different rhythm

The lower part is more composed. A dark rim accentuates the neck, and green and blue patterns are repeated around the rounded body. Stripes divide the design into several layers. They draw the eye differently than the free-flowing outlines of the flowers.

The pattern remains painterly: the lines vary, the paint is visible, and individual shapes shift slightly. At the same time, the repetition maintains the vessel's stability. The composition combines the organised rhythm of the ceramics with the freer expansion of the bouquet.

## Light field and black table

The wide, off-white background gives the flowers space. It contains its own variations—creamy, bluish, and grey brushstrokes—and in some places approaches the light petals. The boundary between the subject and its surroundings becomes softer, maintaining the legibility of the whole.

The black table creates the lower support. The light vase and a few yellow touches on the left are particularly noticeable. The large dark mass balances the open top of the composition. Light and dark work together with the form of the subject.

## Still Life within the Artist’s Wider Exploration

Valikhodjaev addresses various artistic challenges: observing people in the Namangan series, exploring red in RED, and autonomous forms in "New View." In his floral still life, a range of questions is explored through texture, contrast, and the nature of brushstrokes. This familiar theme offers unique opportunities for experimentation.

Compare the overall view with a fragment of flowers, then examine the vase's ornamentation. Returning to the larger view will allow you to more clearly perceive the connection between the two rhythms. The bouquet, the vase, and the surroundings gain expressiveness through the way they are painted and connected on the painting's surface.

## "Bouquets": several vessels and a common rhythm

In "Bouquets" (2008), several vases stand side by side. Their heights, widths, and patterns vary; white daisies and darker flowers form a unified group. The gaze contrasts the free-flowing contours of the inflorescences with the more stable forms of ceramics. The painting demonstrates how a single floral motif allows for multiple rhythms within a single canvas.

The light surface of the table leaves space between the vessels, while fallen petals and small traces of colour connect the lower part to the bouquets. The still life unfolds as a holistic composition: not only the flowers are significant, but also the spacing between the vases, the weight of the dark bouquet, and the distribution of white throughout the composition.

## "Daisies in a Ceramic Vase": Repetition and Difference

In a separate still life from 2008, a single painted vase holds a tall bouquet of daisies. The light-coloured flowers follow the general motif but are rotated unevenly; the yellow centres vary in position and surroundings. Blue painted elements and a dark table provide denser accents in the lower portion.

Compare this bouquet with the roses in the details shown above. In both, brushstrokes contribute to the creation of the flower, but the daisies create a different pattern of repetition. This comparison reveals the diversity of the still life series: the artist is interested not in a single striking bouquet, but in different relationships between colour, ceramic form, and painterly texture.

## Still Life as an Ongoing Exploration of Colour

Writing in San’at in 2012, Oksana Kadysheva wrote about Valikhodjaev's floral painting as a long-standing passion, linking it to a search for rhythm and harmony. In the series presented here, this interest is revealed through various settings. Several vessels in "Bouquets" require a broad grouping; one tall vase with daisies requires a coordinated vertical movement and a stable base. The same genre presents different compositional challenges.

The ceramics add their own distinctive character. The blue pattern on the vase is juxtaposed with yellow flower centres; the dark rim of the vessel supports the thinner stems. The flower and the painted surface remain recognisable, yet they integrate into the overall pictorial rhythm. One can begin with a single white petal, then move on to the adjacent flower and the overall form of the group. This sequence makes the still life a rich visual experience, even without a complex everyday subject.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
