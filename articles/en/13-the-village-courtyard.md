A red wall, a yellow roof, a light window frame: Valikhodjaev's rural landscape retains a recognisable sense of everyday life and simultaneously reveals itself as a study in colour and composition.

## The courtyard as a space of everyday life

"Rural Courtyard" (2013) brings the viewer closer to a lived-in place. Architecture and small everyday details create an environment of human presence. This motif provides a starting point for comparison: other archival works allow us to trace how the artist restructures the rural landscape while maintaining attention to the wall, roof, road, and people.

For a precise discussion, it's important to distinguish between the titles. "Varzik. Rural Landscape" specifies the location in the archival caption itself; "Rural Courtyard" contains no such clarification. It cannot be attributed to Miraki without further information. The various works are included within the general scope of rural motifs, but are not confined to a single locality.

## "Visiting": Meeting at the Red Wall

In the 2010 painting "Visiting," a wide red wall occupies a significant portion of the foreground. The white window frame and the dark opening next to it provide clear geometric supports. Above the wall, the roof's framework opens up: subtle diagonals contrast with the large planes of colour, adding complexity to the architecture.

Two figures stand near the entrance. The light head and headscarf of one stand out against the dark and red surroundings, while the second figure is discernible through more subdued areas. The title directs attention to the encounter, but the relationship between the figures depicted is not explained by the archival caption. Their presence is significant as part of the composition: it alters the scale of the large wall and imbues the architecture with human content.

## Yellow background and black diagonals

A yellow field unfolds behind the house, and dark silhouettes and cooler areas separate it from the nearby buildings. Red therefore resonates alongside light, dark, and cool colours. Its expressiveness is determined by its proximity, not just its own saturation.

The roof's slope and the diagonals of the wooden frame direct the eye upward. Then the white window and the vertical position of the people return it to the stable supports. This meeting of directions reveals a familiar challenge for Valikhodjaev: how to create movement in the painting while maintaining the coherence of the large colour scheme.

## Varzik: A Small Figure in a Broad Landscape

In "Varzik. Rural Landscape" (2013), a small figure stands in front of a long red wall. Its light colour is noticeable even at its relatively small size. The figure gives the wall, gate, and trees a clear scale; the rural landscape ceases to be perceived as an abstract system of colour rectangles.

Beyond the wall, a light-coloured house and yellow roofs are visible, juxtaposed with green trees. The tall, narrow verticals on the left and the more rounded crowns on the right create a different repetition. The horizontal extension of the architecture holds these forms together. The red field below meets the green almost directly, and the yellow introduces additional relationships between them.

## Returning Home: A Glimpse Beside the Gate

Another rural motif is explored in "Returning from Work" (2011). At first glance, the red slopes, white roofs, and blue tractor catch the eye. Upon closer inspection, a small dark figure appears near the house, near the light-coloured gate, as if peering out from behind the wall. This small everyday detail is preserved within the larger colour schemes.

Such details change the way we read the landscape. Large masses of colour remain its foundation, but within them, human action is revealed. It's impossible to determine from the painting who exactly this figure is seeing or what is happening between the characters. What we can discern is something else: this small presence transforms the architectural boundary into a place of potential encounter.

## Miraki and freedom of colour

In "Miraki," red occupies a broad surface, a blue stripe runs alongside, and a light wall separates the spaces. This work is reproduced here as a separate example. Its colour palette helps compare different ways of combining a recognisable landscape with the expressiveness of a large splash of colour.

Rural motifs reveal the diversity of Valikhodjaev's paintings. Sometimes he brings the viewer closer to the courtyard and the encounter at the house, sometimes he retreats to a panoramic view, and in others he makes the relationships between red, green, and blue his primary concern. The exploration of form and composition continues through observation of the everyday environment, and the small human gesture maintains its place within the larger pictorial structure.

## Architecture and a Quiet Human Presence

The house in these paintings is easily recognisable through a few defining elements: the roof, the window, the opening, the wall plane. But each element gains weight from its position. The light roof in "Returning from Work" stands out against the dark sky and red slope; in "Varzik," a long wall connects disparate trees and buildings. Architecture allows the artist to alternate between stable geometry and more free-flowing natural contours.

A small figure changes our sense of scale. Near the gate, we can imagine the distance a person would walk; near the wall, the possibility of action arises. The artist preserves detail within large masses of colour. Therefore, the rural series is interesting both as a search for expressive colour and as an observation of a lived-in environment. Following the panorama, the gaze discovers a courtyard, a gate, a human presence—and the space gradually acquires an everyday content.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
