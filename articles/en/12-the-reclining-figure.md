A light-coloured reclining figure takes up almost the entire width of "Higher Harmony." She is surrounded by two large fields of colour, green and reddish-brown; a dark line runs along the edge of her body. While there are few details, every turn of the silhouette is immediately noticeable. Two other works by the artist in this genre are shown alongside this work: "Sleeping" and "Nude."

"Higher Harmony" and "Sleeping" are from 2007; "Nude" is from 2010. Three archival reproductions allow us to compare how contour, pose, and surrounding field alter the image of a nude.

## Reclining figure and horizontal line of the canvas

In "Higher Harmony," the body develops along a horizontal line. The elongated form occupies most of the surface, but its movement is constantly changing: the flat back gives way to a curve, the waistline narrows the silhouette, and the bent legs introduce a new lift. The pose is formed by the sequence of these turns. The eye perceives the figure immediately and can also follow it gradually, moving along the outer edge.

This solution lends weight to the empty spaces around the subject. A wide green field remains above the back, while a darker, warmer space is beneath the body. Near the knees and the lower edge, the distance between the silhouette and the canvas's edges changes. These spaces contribute to the composition: they give the elongated form space, support its direction, and enhance certain curves.

## What does the dark outline do?

The dark line helps to follow the flow of the pose. It's clear enough to hold the form next to large areas of colour, but its expressiveness depends on its direction: a long, smooth section is perceived differently than a short, broken line. With the restrained internal modelling of the body, it's the edge that communicates the figure's position most clearly. It connects the individual parts into a single, extended movement.

The outline also interacts with the internal lines. A short dark trace within a light mass denotes a turn or articulation, without breaking the body into numerous small details. The distinction between the outer outline and the internal mark preserves the integrity of the figure. This economy of design requires a precise placement of accents: a small line gains significance through the space it occupies within a large, light field.

## Light body among rich colour

The light figure is particularly noticeable between the deep green and reddish-brown. These colours surround her on both sides and create different relationships: the cool green enhances the warmth of the flesh tone, while the dark lower field emphasises the lightness. Thanks to the large, sparsely divided areas, the viewer easily discerns the main masses of the painting. The colour lends a certain strength to the pose even before examining the details.

At the same time, the light surface of the body does not have to be read as a single, unchanging tone. Subtle differences are visible near the contour and within the form; their effect depends on the surrounding colour. The nude allows one to focus on these relationships especially clearly, as clothing and the narrative of everyday life do not distract from the design, the extent of the colour patch, and the nature of the colour juxtaposition.

## "Sleeping": contour and suspended movement

In "Sleeping," a light-coloured reclining figure is placed in a greyish setting. A black line accentuates the curves of the body and changes thickness as it twists. The pose is drawn into an elongated silhouette, but a raised knee disrupts the simple horizontal line. A small reddish round object is noticeable nearby: its size is small, but its colour gains weight among the subdued tones.

The light plane beneath the figure is separated from the darker upper field. This boundary prevents the body from hanging indefinitely: it has a visual anchor. The title indicates the character's state but does not reveal the model's name or the circumstances of its creation. The analysis relies on the visible organization of the form.

## "Nude": a figure in a cool palette

In "Nude" from 2010, the figure is shown against a bluish background. Dark hair and the finer contour of the pale body create varying degrees of contrast. The pose extends to the right edge; much of the surrounding space remains relatively clear. In the general view, it is particularly noticeable how a slight change in line alters the readability of the shoulder, back, and head position.

Compared to "Sleeping," the cool colour becomes a more independent participant in the image. The light body and surroundings are still closely related, but the relationship takes on a different meaning. The size, according to the archival caption, is 30 x 43 cm; the intimate format does not preclude meaningful work with the colour field.

## Three versions of one artistic problem

"Higher Harmony," "Sleeping," and "Nude" are united by their attention to the form and movement of the reclining body, but are handled differently. The rich surroundings, subdued greyish environment, and bluish field alter the weight of the light figure and the pace of viewing. The dark outline can become dominant or give way to a more subtle transition.

These works reveal a distinct side of Valikhodjaev's work alongside bazaar compositions, portraits, RED, and abstraction. The nude becomes material for exploring form: the artist compares pose, line, and the space around the body, finding different expressive solutions within a familiar genre.

## Looking closely at the figure’s contour

In each of the three works, it's worth tracing the point where the body meets the surrounding field. In "Higher Harmony," the dark line creates a confident and extended boundary. In "Sleeping," the contour's twists are noticeable against a background of restrained grey tones. In the small "Nude," the light form is closer in tone to the blue surroundings, forcing attention to focus on the softer transitions. The very degree of separation of the body from space varies.

This choice influences the pace of perception. A clear edge allows one to quickly grasp the pose; closely related tones require a slower gaze. The size of the canvas also alters the encounter: an intimate work is viewed from close range, while a large format reveals the expanse of the silhouette. Therefore, the nude genre here encompasses several tasks simultaneously—the drawing of bodily form, the relationship between light and dark, and the extent of the open colour field. The human figure becomes the starting point for exploration, which develops within the specific structure of each work.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.

## The figure painting in the exhibition room

The archival photographs return the painted figure to the scale of an exhibition. Beside the artist and visitors, the canvases become physical objects: their height, the breadth of a red field and the curve of a pale silhouette can be measured against a person. These views complement the reproductions above, showing how a viewer approaches a painting and how the figure occupies space on the gallery wall.
