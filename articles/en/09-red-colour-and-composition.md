Two red horses stand in an open blue field, with a small, light-coloured semicircle above them. "Red Horses" (2005) demonstrates that colour can define an image without a detailed description of the location. This earlier work helps open a conversation about Valikhodjaev's artistic interest in red. Its inclusion in the RED exhibition is not confirmed by available materials; documented RED landscapes are shown below; the video at the end presents New View, another direction in his exploration.

## Red Horses: Silhouette and Colour Field

The large figure stretches across the canvas almost from edge to edge. Below it is a smaller horse; its back follows the direction of the upper one, but the proportions and position of its legs are not the same. Therefore, the repetition creates a connection between the two figures without turning them into a single symbol. A narrow space of blue between them helps distinguish their contours.

Here, the red isn't dissolved into the complex landscape. It's concentrated in two large silhouettes and gains strength next to the clear blue field. The light semicircle at the top is small in area, but it disrupts the two-colour scheme and creates another point of attention. The texture of the paint layer is noticeable even in the reproduction: the surface retains brush marks and doesn't appear mechanically smooth.

The comparison with the landscapes shown below is particularly useful. There, the red field could become a road or part of a rural development; here, it belongs to two figures. The subject of the image changes, but the question remains: what area does the dominant colour occupy, how does its border move, and how does the adjacent colour alter the perception of form.

In the central landscape of the RED series photograph, a broad red spot curves between houses and trees. A blue stripe runs alongside, light walls form angular sections, and dark trees anchor the edges of the composition. The location remains recognisable, but the first strong impression is created by the relationships between colour and form.

In Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works, these relationships deserve special attention. In figurative painting, colour plays a role in depicting the world of objects; in abstraction, large planes and geometric elements acquire independent expressiveness. A comparison of the landscapes from the RED series with the paintings in the "New View" exhibition helps us understand how colour connects the different directions of his practice.

RED is associated with a passionate exploration of the colour red. Valikhodjaev is interested in its expressive power, its ability to hold a composition together, and its ability to alter the perception of depicted space. Red becomes the leading theme of the painterly experience: the area it occupies, how its boundaries are structured, and what happens when juxtaposed with blue, light, or dark areas are important. Through these relationships, the artist explores different states of a single colour.

## Colour patch as the basis of composition

A colour patch is a surface area perceived as a solid form. It has size, direction, and boundaries. In a landscape, such a spot might coincide with an image of a road, a wall, or a tree crown; however, it also functions as part of the overall structure of the painting. Its expressiveness depends on its relationship to neighbouring areas.

In the central work of the RED series, red occupies a significant portion of the lower space. The curve of its border directs the eye into the depths and connects the foreground with the architecture. The richness of the colour gives this area great visual weight. Even a small light or dark detail nearby becomes noticeable thanks to the contrast.

This solution allows the landscape to maintain its integrity despite its many subject elements. Houses, trees, figures, and small details are integrated into larger colour relationships. The eye first perceives the main masses, then discerns their inner content. The composition unfolds from the overall impression to a closer examination.

A separate archival reproduction of "Miraki" allows one to see the painting's surface and the boundaries of the colour patches more clearly than the overall appearance of the exhibition. Red occupies a wide space between the houses and trees; a blue stripe curves along it, and the light wall creates a sharp, angular boundary.

## Contour and character of form

The boundary of a colour patch determines how it interacts with the surrounding forms. A gentle curve creates one direction, a sharp angle another. In the landscape shown, a wide red surface is juxtaposed with the stepped and angular contours of light-coloured walls. This combination gives the space both movement and stability.

Dark trees and details enhance individual edges. Their silhouettes vary in height and density; they help balance the light architecture and the bright foreground. The outline here has its own expressiveness: it determines how well the object is read and how its shape fits into the overall composition.

In an artistically simplified landscape, the outlines of objects can become clearer and more conventional. This helps emphasise the chosen character of the location. The viewer recognises the architecture and vegetation, simultaneously perceiving them as a system of lines and colour masses. The decorative structure emerges from the coherence of these solutions.

## Colour: the juxtaposition of warm and cold

The red and blue in the central landscape enhance the contrast between adjacent areas. Warm ochre and orange hues support one side of the colour scheme, while the light walls and dark silhouettes introduce other relationships. Each colour gains its expressiveness from its surroundings. Therefore, colour should be considered within the entire composition.

The size of the spot also matters. A narrow blue stripe can be very vibrant next to a wide red field; a small white figure can stand out among saturated colours. Visual weight depends on size, contrast, and position simultaneously. These relationships help the artist direct attention without describing each object in detail.

The photograph features several individual paintings from the RED series, including "Mirakli" from 2016, mentioned in the exhibition report caption. Their juxtaposition allows for a comparison of recurring colour schemes. Red maintains a prominent role, and its size, shape, and relationship to the architecture vary from piece to piece.

## Transition to geometric abstraction

In the paintings in the "New View" exhibition, red, yellow, white, grey, and black areas form geometric compositions. Here, expressiveness is built directly on the relationships between planes. A narrow stripe, a large rectangle, or a diagonal line gain meaning through their position and relationship to the rest of the surface.

A comparison with landscapes helps us discern a common set of artistic goals. In both cases, large masses, clear boundaries, and a strong accent of colour are essential. In landscape, these solutions reveal the subject matter; in abstraction, they organise an independent pictorial image. Different representational methods allow us to see the meaning of colour in a new way.

When examining works, it's helpful to move from the overall picture to the details: first, notice the distribution of the main spots, then their boundaries and minor accents. A reproduction allows you to study the compositional structure, while an original reveals the texture and true power of colour. In both cases, a careful look reveals how colour becomes a means of constructing form.

In "New View," the artist's curiosity is directed toward abstraction: independent forms and colour fields allow for a different construction of the image. A juxtaposition with RED reveals a common research orientation. Valikhodjaev continues to question the force of a colour accent, the nature of the border, and the balance of composition, discovering new solutions in various artistic directions. Figurative and abstract works retain their independence within this exploration.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.

## RED: painting before an audience

Two photographs of the masterclass accompanying RED in Tashkent in 2017 bring the discussion of colour into practice. Visitors gather close to the easel and follow the sequence of actions. Speed is only one part of the demonstration: the silhouette, the extent of a colour patch and the intervals between forms become visible decisions.

The Embassy of Uzbekistan’s report on the exhibition records 31 works and a masterclass for visitors. The photographs open the process to view: the position of the hand, the palette and the emerging image can be compared. The investigation of red takes shape through its neighbouring tones and the organisation of the entire canvas.
