An everyday scene becomes a painting when the artist discovers its inner structure: the arrangement of figures, the character of gesture, the relationship of colour. Ibragim Valikhodjaev's interest in the lives of his contemporaries combines with attention to composition and colour. Recognizable images of people and places are revealed through artistic generalization, which imbues the ordinary episode with expressiveness.

In 2024, a solo exhibition, "In Silence," was held in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, at the Mondoir Gallery. Curated by Zahra Habibizad, also known as Zara, the exhibition introduced Valikhodjaev's paintings to an international audience and allowed for a glimpse into how themes of everyday Uzbek life inform a broader conversation about humanity, memory, and artistic language.

The Dubai exhibition can also be seen as a meeting of different artistic directions. In his Namangan series, Valikhodjaev was captivated by the bazaar, people, and everyday life; in **[RED](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/colour-gives-form-looking-closely-at.html>)**, by the expressive potential of red; and in abstraction, by the independent relationships of forms and colour planes. These interests help us understand the breadth of his practice and his constant desire to find new solutions within painting.

## Everyday life as a theme of painting

A genre painting emphasises people in their familiar surroundings: their activities, interactions, postures, and relationships. In such a work, a single gesture can convey more about the scene's character than a detailed description of the location. Figures form a group, the distances between them create a sense of closeness or isolation, and the composition helps highlight the main points.

In Valikhodjaev's bazaar paintings, featured in illustrated publications about his work, the human environment is rich and multi-faceted. White scarves, dark silhouettes, patterned skullcaps, and brightly coloured clothing create recognisable details. At the same time, the image is based on large-scale relationships: the repetition of figures, the alternation of light and dark, and the distribution of warm and cool tones.

This approach preserves cultural specificity and gives the viewer space for their own perception. One can first notice national details, then the relationships between people, and finally the colour scheme of the entire scene. These levels gradually complement each other. An acquaintance with the life depicted on the canvas begins with an observation of its pictorial structure.

## Colour and artistic generalization

Expressive colour helps highlight the character of a scene. Red clothing can become a supporting accent among more sedate figures; repeated white areas can unite several groups; a dark outline can add clarity to the silhouette. Colour works in conjunction with the composition, directing attention and defining the overall emotional tone of the work.

Artistic generalization means selecting essential features. A conventional form, a magnified spot of colour, or a reduced detail help to create a coherent image. The viewer recognises a person, a street, or a landscape, and then sees how the artist has transformed the observation. In this work, the individuality of the pictorial language emerges.

To understand the paintings, it's helpful to consider both the subject matter and the degree of its transformation. Where is the figure described in more detail? Where does it remain almost a silhouette? How do the outlines of objects fit into the overall plane? These questions make the discussion of colour vibrancy more precise: it focuses on how colour helps construct form, space, and the relationships between characters.

"Skullcap Bazaar" offers an opportunity to examine this language more closely. In this reproduction from the artist's archive, white scarves form large, light shapes; black outlines, a red background, and yellow sections of clothing tie them together into a dense composition. A recognisable everyday motif is preserved within a clear decorative framework.

## An exhibition spanning different periods

A Gulf Today review on June 26, 2024, reported that the exhibition brought together works from 1992 to 2024, including those loaned by private collectors. This range allows the exhibition to be viewed as an encounter with various stages of the artist's career. Works typically held in separate collections are given the opportunity to engage in a dialogue within a single gallery space.

A comparison of figurative works and later abstraction reveals several directions of artistic exploration. In the former, colour is associated with people and objects; in the latter, large planes and geometric relationships take on an independent role. Common qualities can be found between these movements: clarity of colour accents, rhythmic organization, and attention to scale.

The comprehensive exhibition also allows us to see how differently a single artist approaches painting. A small image requires close examination, while a large canvas requires a different scope. A common artistic path is revealed through a variety of approaches. Therefore, an exhibition of different periods is important for understanding the artist as a working, developing artist.

The transition between narrative painting and abstraction reveals different modes of exploration. In one case, the starting point is the observation of people and the nature of a familiar environment; in the other, the power of colour, rhythm, and surface structure. Their juxtaposition allows us to see the development of the artist's language: the themes and degree of generalization shift, while attention to compositional and colour relationships continues to connect the works.

## Meeting a new audience

For a viewer in Dubai, everyday Uzbek life can be a new cultural experience. The first impression is formed by the visible: faces, clothing, objects, colours. Then, more general human connections emerge—a recognition of communication, labor, and the movement of urban life. Painting creates the opportunity for such a connection through concrete images.

The curator's work helps shape the viewing experience: which works will be displayed next to each other, where the viewing will begin, and which periods will be juxtaposed. In the case of In Silence, this context should be considered alongside the individual paintings. The exhibition addresses the audience as a whole, and the chosen juxtapositions provide additional context for perceiving each work.

The video presentation and publications about the exhibition preserve its public history. They allow visitors to explore the gallery environment and continue their acquaintance with the artist through his biography, other exhibitions, and reproductions. For a more detailed reading, it's worth returning to the paintings themselves: in the relationship between figures and colour, the cultural narrative receives its artistic form.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
