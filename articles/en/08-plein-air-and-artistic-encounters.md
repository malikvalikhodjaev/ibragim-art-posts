En plein air, the artist works in a changing world. Light shifts across the surface, shadows lengthen, people enter and leave the field of view. Observation requires a quick selection: which relationships form the basis of the future painting, what should be preserved, what details can remain beyond the canvas. This work combines immediate impressions with a trained ability to select and compose.

Ibragim Valikhodjaev participated as a painter in plein air painting in Turkey in 2023, and in 2026 in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, and Shabran, Azerbaijan. Also in 2026, he curated the Andijan Fine Arts Festival. His work as an artist and as a curator offer different perspectives on artistic encounters and constitute independent parts of his professional experience. In 2012, Ibragim Valikhodjaev also participated as a painter in plein air painting in Montenegro.

For Valikhodjaev, plein air is part of a constant search for new artistic directions and solutions. A different environment offers unexpected colour, a different character of light, and new relationships of form. A passion for observation combines with an exploration of composition: the artist chooses what will be the main focus of the painting and how to convey the impression through his own artistic language.

## Working from life: the main impression

Plein air is working outdoors, directly in front of a subject. The artist may choose to focus on landscapes, architecture, or figures in the surrounding environment. The initial task is to identify key relationships: where the light is, what masses form the composition, how warm and cool areas are related.

A study allows you to capture what you've seen in a compact, coherent solution. Integrity is especially important here: large colour relationships must be maintained while individual details are refined. Changing light requires relating each new stage of the work to the initial impression. Therefore, plein air painting develops the ability to see the overall structure of a motif and maintain it on the canvas.

In the first video above, shot in Maltepe in 2023, Valikhodjaev works in front of a cityscape. The camera allows for a juxtaposition of the subject and the painting's surface. An artistic selection occurs between them: the complex environment is given a limited format, the object forms are unified, and the colour areas become more defined. Thus, observation transforms into a painterly composition.

## From place to painting

Each location has a characteristic scale and spatial arrangement. An architectural view suggests verticals, horizontals, and perspective directions; a natural motif offers freer contours. The artist's position determines the relationship between the foreground and background. Even a slight rotation alters the composition and distribution of the main masses.

Timing is also important. Morning light highlights some surfaces, evening light highlights others; cloudiness can bring tonal relationships closer together. These changes influence the colour scheme of the future work. Colour is linked to specific observation conditions, and painterly convention allows the artist to express the perceived character of the motif.

Travelling and participating in plein-air painting sessions offer new challenges to an already established artistic language. The artist encounters different architecture, lighting, and spatial relationships. The painting reveals the qualities of the place he highlights and how he incorporates them into his own compositional practice. This experience expands his powers of observation and choice.

This curiosity manifests itself in various aspects of his practice. In the Namangan series, the starting point was the people and everyday life of the bazaar; in RED, an exploration of the colour red; and in New View, the possibilities of abstraction. Plein air adds to this exploration a direct encounter with changing nature. Communication with other artists provides the opportunity to compare solutions and discover new questions for one's own work.

## Plein air as a professional meeting

Group work allows for comparison of solutions emerging from similar contexts. One participant will focus on a space, another on a colour accent, and a third will select a small fragment. This comparison demonstrates how diverse artistic perspectives on a single environment can be. A shared path doesn't necessarily lead to identical paintings.

In 2026, Valikhodjaev participated in a plein air painting session in Azerbaijan. In the second video above, people standing nearby observe the process: they see new spots appear and the composition develop. The work becomes a pretext for a conversation about painting, and the viewer gets the opportunity to observe professional decisions as they emerge.

The participants themselves find the exchange of experiences and the opportunity to see different ways of working with nature particularly valuable. Conversations can focus on the chosen perspective, colour, format, or level of generalization. The shared environment creates the conditions for such exchange, and each completed work retains its own distinctive artistic expression.

## Curatorial experience and artistic environment

Curating the Andijan festival represents a different aspect of Valikhodjaev's professional work. Here, attention is focused on the program and interactions among participants, as well as on how various works and practices can be presented together. The curator helps build meaningful connections and conditions for public engagement with art.

Experience of personal participation allows one to understand artistic encounters from the inside: how the work develops, the benefits of observing colleagues, and the importance of conversation around the works. This experience can be useful in curatorial practice, where one must consider both the artist's autonomy and the overall structure of the event.

Plein-air painting sessions in Turkey, Samarkand, and Shabran, as well as a festival in Andijan, showcase several forms of professional interaction. In some projects, Valikhodjaev creates his own paintings, while in others, he works as a curator. Together, they reveal art as an ongoing practice: observation, working with form, sharing experiences, and meeting the viewer.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. ## Plein air begins with looking

Plein air begins well before the first brushstroke, with a walk and a choice of viewpoint. These photographs show a village lane, an enclosed courtyard, a rocky slope and autumn trees. One motif can be read as an alternation of pale walls and dark openings; another as a road moving between buildings. The painter chooses both what to depict and the arrangement of broad masses that will hold the future picture together.

These working photographs complement the landscapes by restoring a sense of scale, uneven light and surface texture. Walls, roofs, a strip of earth and a tree crown supply material for colour relationships. In the finished painting, observation may become freer and more condensed; its connection with the motif remains in the organisation of space. The photographs have no established location or year: their appearance alone cannot assign them to a particular plein-air programme in Miraki, Montenegro or Azerbaijan.
