# Learning to See: Ibragim Valikhodjaev on a Life in Art

[https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post\_179.html](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post_179.html>)

Life and conversations about art

From Rahim Ahmedov's studio to the canvases filling a museum hall, the Uzbek artist's journey is one of observation, questioning, and new exploration.

The biographical portrait is based on published interviews, exhibition history, and materials from the artist’s archive.

"Ijod formulasi," June 9, 2026, Madaniyat va Ma'rifat TV channel. The video above is in Uzbek; the links below lead to the relevant excerpts from the conversation.

In the program "Ijod formulasi" (The Formula for Creativity), the conversation begins with a visit to Ibragim Valikhodjaev's studio. Works from various periods stand side by side: a market scene, a red landscape, a large abstract composition. Behind them lies a single creative life, but in each work, the artist tackles a new challenge. This meeting in the studio helps us understand the most important thing: Valikhodjaev's curiosity constantly opens up new directions for his exploration.

He is a painter and sculptor, organiser and curator, gallery founder, and mentor. A common question runs through all these activities: how to maintain a fresh perspective? In published conversations, experience is presented as an opportunity to continue research, experiment, and find new expressive solutions.

## The teacher opens the way, the artist searches for his language

Valikhodjaev was born in Namangan and received his professional training in Tashkent, in the workshop of Master Rahim Ahmedov. In a 2017 interview, he described his teacher as the person who shaped his understanding of colour. This is an important connection: behind the freedom of his painting lies rigorous training that provided the foundation for an independent artistic language.

In a television interview, the artist distinguishes between drawing and the development of an artist. Training opens a vast world of possibilities, but finding one's own direction within it is up to the artist. This journey can be traced through his work, through portraits, still lifes, landscapes, and multi-figure genre compositions.

## The Bazaar Is More than a Subject

The bazaar theme captivated the artist while he was working on his diploma thesis and long remained one of the main themes of his work. The Namangan series offers rich compositional material: encounters, exchange of goods, conversations, and gestures. White scarves, embroidered skullcaps, and colourful clothing form recurring accents, while individual characters are integrated into the overall rhythm of everyday life.

In "Eastern Suite," the gaze shifts from small human stories to a broad compositional structure. Gesture is important as an expression of character and simultaneously as a change of form, altering the distances between adjacent figures. Observation here becomes painting: people's lives are artistically organised through colour, grouping, and rhythm. The artist is captivated by both the marketplace world itself and the challenge of bringing its many voices together into a coherent canvas.

## Colour becomes the subject of research

For the 2017 RED exhibition, red became a distinct artistic focus. In an interview with nuz.uz, Valikhodjaev explains that in his work, there are periods of particular attention to one colour. Red intrigued him for its emotional power and the complexity of its combination with other colours.

This is a search for colouristic relationships. What part of the surface does colour occupy? What happens at the boundary with the adjacent tone? How can the structure of the landscape be preserved while allowing colour greater autonomy? In these works, red plays a role in the construction of the image and determines its emotional resonance.

[Video above · 03:06](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uQzIBpYBXD8&t=186s>) · [nuz.uz · 18 April 2017](<https://nuz.uz/2017/04/18/ibragim-valihodzhaev-hudozhnik-dolzhen-byt-ubeditelnym/>)

## The language found leaves room for change

“I always try to see familiar things in a new, unusual way,” the artist said in a 2017 interview (translated from Russian). This idea helps connect his genre painting with his subsequent turn to abstraction. In "New View," the image is constructed through large colour fields, contours, and geometric relationships. The compositional connections that united the characters in the market scene now emerge between independent forms.

The exploration of colour and composition continues: the subject and scale of the painting change, and new ways of connecting parts of the canvas emerge. In a televised interview, Valikhodjaev emphasises that his recognisable style is no excuse for stopping. Each subsequent work should open the door to a new artistic approach.

## A new place changes the encounter with the work

Travelling provides new context for this exploration. In 2012, Valikhodjaev participated as a painter in a plein-air gathering in Montenegro, and then exhibited the works he created there in Tashkent. His plein air work also includes Turkey, Uzbekistan, and Azerbaijan. In Hulan, China, he participated as a sculptor, translating a composition into metal for a floating sculpture project.

The solo exhibition "Sun on Snow" at the Yu Shun Museum in Harbin, China, in 2026 gave his large-scale canvases a new dimension. The video above demonstrates the impact of the room: from a distance, the overall structure is discernible, but upon closer inspection, a distinct field of colour takes up a significant portion of the viewer's field of vision. The artist's works are held in this museum, in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality in Istanbul, Turkey, and in a private collection in Moscow, Russia, along with other public and private collections.

## Experience becomes a shared workspace

Valikhodjaev combines his own work with organising and curating eight international sculpture symposia in Turkey and Uzbekistan. This involves inviting artists and creating conditions for their creativity. In an interview with Kultura.uz in 2024, he particularly emphasised the knowledge and experience acquired by Uzbek sculptors, especially his younger colleagues.

A desire to share experience is evident in gallery work and teaching. The second gallery, Valikhodjaev Gallery House, opened on August 5, 2021. Painting demonstrations and classes with students allow for close-up observation of professional work: how the artist begins a painting, changes a section, and evaluates the result. Organising a meeting, taking part in a plein-air gathering, and creating one's own work are different roles, each with its own responsibilities.

## Keep Looking, Keep Making

The biography allows us to trace exhibitions and professional assignments; interviews reveal the inner workings of his work. Valikhodjaev speaks of effort, doubt, the search for a concept, and the difficulty of making a work appear outwardly simple. These reflections take us back to the studio, where accumulated experience must be transformed into a concrete solution on the canvas.

His journey continues in his works: in marketplace people, the changing resonance of red, the construction of abstract canvases, and the encounter of sculpture with public space. These various movements are united by a habit of exploration—of looking anew and finding a different pictorial response.

[Video above · 27:27](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uQzIBpYBXD8&t=1647s>)

## The composition connects different years

In a 2012 profile article for San'at, Oksana Kadysheva described Valikhodjaev's journey through several directions: his early, restrained palette, market scenes, Montenegrin landscapes, and floral still lifes. At the centre of her reading is the artist's ability to transform observation into composition. This context helps us see his creative life more comprehensively: a new direction emerges from yet another question about the image, while previous themes continue to coexist.

In the archival works, this curiosity takes various forms. The pale-toned “Jugs” rely on tonal nuances, "Red Horses" on the relationship of large silhouettes to a blue field, and “The Harbour at Perast” on the alternation of spatial planes. The differences between them are significant. Together, they allow us to understand artistic exploration as a long-term practice of choice: where to direct attention, what degree of detail to preserve, how to impart a new resonance to the form.

## Passing on experience: a masterclass in Xi’an

In September 2025, Valikhodjaev led a painting masterclass for students at Xi’an Academy of Fine Arts in China. He took part in the 14–19 September visit associated with CASIE’s ‘One Belt, One Road — Central Asia’ programme as an artist and chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. The composition, completed in one hour and twenty minutes, was donated to the Academy. The masterclass video below follows the development of the image, from its first lines to the relationships between areas of colour and the finished composition.
