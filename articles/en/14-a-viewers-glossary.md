How can we describe a painting more precisely than “bright” or “beautiful”? Artistic terms help us name what we notice: the tilt of a head, the breadth of a white shape, the repetition of light-coloured scarves in the crowd. Their meaning becomes clearer when viewed alongside a specific work. Ibragim Valikhodjaev's paintings offer a variety of examples—a portrait, a market scene, an architectural landscape, and an abstract composition.

These ten concepts can be read in order or selected based on interest. Each term is associated with a small action of the gaze: finding, comparing, tracing. This discussion leaves room for both the painting's content and the artist's organization of the surface.

## 1. Subject

The subject is the action, scene or situation depicted. In "Eastern Suite," it is revealed by the people gathered at the market, the white scarves, and the small objects in the foreground. It's enough to pick out a single gesture to begin to understand the scene. Then you can see how this episode relates to the surrounding figures.

## 2. Motif

A motif is a recognisable element that the artist develops in different works. In marketplace paintings, this element is a white scarf; in landscapes, it is a house or a winding path. Repeatedly revisiting the motif allows for variations in format, scale, and colour relationships, finding a new take on a familiar theme.

## 3. Silhouette

A silhouette is the general outline of a figure or object. A white scarf can be recognised by its broad, light contour even before one sees its face. In "Rhythms of Houses," the dark figures of the people are also read primarily as elongated forms. The silhouette helps combine image recognition with compositional clarity.

## 4. Composition

Composition connects the parts of a work into a whole. In "Namangan Suite," the placement of groups, the overlapping of figures, and the movement along the horizontal format are important. Each character occupies a specific space, and repeated light and colour forms connect distant areas. Ask yourself what path through the painting this arrangement suggests.

## 5. Rhythm

Rhythm emerges from repetition and variation. White scarves return at different heights; the tilt of a head alters the movement of the adjacent group. In a geometric composition, angles, arcs, and areas of colour can play a similar role. To sense rhythm, select one element and trace its returns across the surface.

## 6. Colour

A colour scheme is the system of relationships between colours in a painting. In "Miraki," the red field, blue road, light wall, and dark trees are perceived as interconnected. The hue, size, and proximity of each section are all significant. The exploration of red in RED demonstrates how many purposes a single dominant colour can convey.

## 7. Contrast

Contrast is a pronounced difference that enhances the perception of adjacent forms. In a market scene, it's white next to a dark face; in a landscape, it's a light roof next to a richly coloured wall. Contrast can highlight a small object or add weight to a large plane. Its strength depends on the surrounding area.

## 8. Texture

Texture is the visible character of a surface and the applied paint. In the archival photograph of "Rhythms of Houses," one can see the brushwork moving within the white roofs and yellow walls. Even with a clear geometric outline, the surface retains a painterly fluidity. It's best to examine it in front of the original; a high-quality reproduction helps select an area for further attention.

## 9. Format and scale

Format defines the proportions of the image, scale defines the relationship of size, including the painting's encounter with the viewer. The elongated "Namangan Suite" offers a panoramic route. A large work on a museum wall requires a different distance and a longer walk. A video from Harbin helps us see what a person looks like next to the canvas.

## 10. Abstraction

Abstraction reveals the independent relationships of colour, form, and space. In "New View," Valikhodjaev explored this language with curiosity. Geometric forms invite attention to boundaries, direction, and the distribution of visual weight. A familiarity with narrative painting helps us notice how certain compositional concerns are continued in other artistic approaches.

A dictionary becomes useful when it brings us back to the work. Choose two concepts and look at the painting again: for example, silhouette and colour, or format and rhythm. Then the term gains concrete meaning, and the impression is enriched by observations that can be shared with another viewer.

## Putting the Words to Work

The portrait and the marketplace panorama allow us to test the same term in different contexts. In "Portrait of Gulchehra," rhythm is created by the broad leaves, the turn of the head, and the lines of the arms. In "Eastern Suite," it is carried by groups of people and repeating light-coloured scarves. The concept remains the same, but the visual relationships shift. The comparison helps us use artistic vocabulary more precisely: to name the rhythm means to see what exactly is repeated and how the repetition is disrupted.

Colour and tone can also be compared. The white dress in the portrait stands out among the green and blue; in the early "Prayer," the light-coloured figures are closer to the surrounding field. In the first case, the difference is immediately noticeable; in the second, the image emerges gradually. The term becomes useful when we return to the work. It helps us notice the specific, sustain the observation, and explain it to another viewer.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
