In the lead photograph, Ibragim Valikhodjaev stands before a monumental geometric painting. His figure gives a sense of the scale of the painted surface. Red, yellow, white and dark shapes extend along the wall; seeing their relationships and individual passages requires the viewer to change position.

The solo exhibition "Sun on Snow" was held in Harbin, China, at the Yu Shun Museum from February 19 to March 19, 2026. A Chinese [art newspaper](<https://msb.zjol.com.cn/html/2026-02/28/content_4466971.htm?div=-1>) reported on 19 large paintings and a 22.24 x 3-meter canvas. This exhibition presents a particular challenge for the viewer: the perception of the painting becomes part of the movement through the room, and the museum's architecture contributes to the artistic experience.

The scale of the Harbin exhibition underlies an ongoing exploration of colour, form, and composition. For Valikhodjaev, the large format opens up new relationships between the individual spot and the entire canvas, between painting and the viewer's bodily experience. This interest in the possibilities of abstraction was particularly clearly expressed in the "New View" exhibition in Tashkent; in Harbin, it can be explored through the painting’s expanse and its presence in the museum hall.

## A canvas that unfolds as you walk

A long canvas takes time to explore: it cannot be taken in at a single glance. A small painting can be grasped almost instantly; here, the gaze gradually unfolds from one section to the next. As one moves along the wall, some forms approach, while others recede into the periphery of vision. Memorized colour accents connect the previously seen part with the one that lies further away.

In geometric composition, both repetition and variation are important. Rectangular planes are juxtaposed with inclined elements, large fields alternate with smaller sections, and dark lines connect adjacent colours. This sequence maintains the integrity of the large format. The length of the canvas becomes a prerequisite for developing rhythm, alternating tense and calmer sections.

From a distance, numerous details coalesce into large masses of colour. Up close, a different perception emerges: the boundaries of spots, the width of stripes, the meeting of multiple directions. These two scales of observation complement each other. A single fragment reveals the structure of the pictorial language, while the overall view allows one to sense its scope.

## Painting in the architecture of the hall

A museum exhibition always sets the conditions for encountering a work. The height of the ceiling, the free space in front of the wall, and the proximity of other works determine the viewing angle and distance. The photographs published above show a spacious room with a high ceiling. The room’s proportions help us understand the place of the monumental composition in the surrounding space.

A white wall separates the colour surface from the architecture, and the floor line emphasises its expanse. When a person appears in the frame, the size ratio becomes especially striking. The colour fields, which appear compact on a small screen, are comparable to or significantly larger than the human figure in the museum. This creates a sense of immediate presence in front of the work.

Different perspectives reveal the different qualities of a single exhibition. A frontal view more clearly conveys the compositional structure; a side view reveals the length and connection of the canvas to the wall. The overall plan unites the painting, the room, and the visitors. Therefore, museum documentation is important for understanding monumental painting: it preserves information about the space that is not available in a single reproduction.

## How colour structures a large canvas

In a wide format, colour must maintain its expressiveness over a considerable distance. Large red, yellow, and light areas form anchor points. Black and grey elements enhance the edges and create contrasts in lightness. The interplay of these masses helps the eye navigate a surface whose dimensions exceed the typical easel painting format.

Geometry gives colour definition. Each field has a direction, extent, and relationship to its neighbours. A diagonal can draw the eye along the composition; a vertical can halt movement; a wide, bright plane can provide a pause. This is how the spatial structure of the work is formed, which is perceived in conjunction with the real distances of the museum hall.

The title "Sun on Snow" offers the viewer an image of light and contrast. It can be retained in mind by examining the colour relationships, but the detailed symbolism of all the forms should be left open. The main artistic content is already evident in the composition itself: in the alternation of planes, the intensity of colour, and the relationship of scale.

The search here can be traced in specific decisions: how wide to expand the colour field, how to connect distant fragments, where to accelerate the rhythm, and where to leave a pause. A single motif gains varying strength depending on its size and surroundings. Monumental painting allows the artist to experience these relationships on a scale that changes the usual way of encountering a painting.

## An exhibition as a meeting with the artist

The video recording of the opening, linked above, captures another aspect of the event: the speeches, the artist’s remarks, and the guests interacting in front of the works. The museum hall becomes a venue for a public conversation about painting. The figures of visitors provide an idea of ​​the scale of the works, and their presence reminds us that the exhibition exists in the experience of specific individuals.

[The Chinese report](<https://www.sohu.com/a/990524748_121106822>) dates the opening ceremony to February 24, 2026. In Harbin, Valikhodjaev is presented primarily as the author of a solo painting exhibition. His professional work also includes sculpture, teaching, curating, and organising international projects. Here, attention is focused on how his colour and compositional approaches are presented to museum audiences in a monumental format.

The two videos linked above—a short film about the exhibition and a full recording of the opening—are useful in different ways. The first allows one to follow the layout of the halls and view the paintings from several angles. The second conveys the atmosphere and social context of the event. Together, they help present the exhibition as a holistic experience: the work, the space, and the viewer's presence.

## Background to the meeting with the Chinese audience

The Harbin exhibition continues the artist's history of international exhibition encounters. On January 23, 2024, UzA reported on the opening of an exhibition of fine and applied folk art from Uzbekistan in Beijing. Among the artists featured was Ibragim Valikhodjaev. According to the agency, the exhibition included fifty paintings by various artists; the venue was the China National Arts and Crafts Museum – China Intangible Cultural Heritage Museum. Valikhodjaev participated in the Beijing group exhibition; Harbin presented a solo exhibition of his paintings.

The difference in format is essential for perception. A group exhibition allows one to see different artistic voices side by side; a solo exhibition allows one to linger within a single artist's world, comparing their approaches and following the development of a theme. In "Sun on Snow," this focus is supported by the scale of the works. A few meters of surface compel one to return to what one has seen, and the sequence of rooms transforms the introduction to colour and geometry into an extended experience.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
