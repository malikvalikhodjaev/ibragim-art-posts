"Prayer," "Jugs," and "Gaze" explore the varied possibilities of a light, restrained palette. Here, the colour exploration develops through tonal nuances, intervals, and how clearly a form is defined.

## Subtle colour requires different attention

In front of “Prayer”, the eye is first met by a wide, light field. The figures are similar in tone and emerge gradually. Nearby, "Jugs" suggest a different motif—several vessels arranged on a light surface. "Gaze" introduces a clearer architectural boundary and a human figure, only partially revealed to the viewer. Three paintings from the archive expand our understanding of Valikhodjaev's restrained palette.

According to archival captions, "Jugs" and "Gaze" are dated 1997, and "Prayer" is dated 1998. This information pertains to the works named; it does not reduce the artist's entire early practice to a single, uniform period. The nearly monochromatic colour scheme here allows for a variety of subjects and different ways of organising space.

## "Prayer": the image appears gradually

In the lower part of "Prayer," a compact group of light-coloured figures is discernible. Above them, a large space opens up, in which outlines and tonal transitions softly emerge. The relationship between the small group and the expansive surroundings immediately alters the scale of human presence: the action feels like part of a larger, calmer field.

The title gives the work a certain semantic direction. However, the accessible archival caption does not provide the location, the identities of the characters, or a detailed history of the scene. For artistic analysis, the visible elements are sufficient: the proximity of light tones, the size of the figures, the arrangement of the group, and the gradual emergence of form. A contemplative impression arises from these relationships.

## "Jugs": differences within a row

In "Jugs," several light-coloured vessels stand side by side, yet differ in height, width, and neck shape. A narrow, elongated silhouette sits alongside a more rounded one. The repetition of the common theme makes the differences particularly noticeable: the eye compares the adjacent forms and traces the alternation of narrow and wide, high and low.

The nearly white objects are separated from the light surroundings by subtle hues and darker edges. The dark line becomes more pronounced in places, softer in others. This allows the vessel to remain legible and not become a uniformly outlined sign. The simple composition reveals the complex work with spacing, proportions, and the degree of border definition.

## Tone and surface instead of strong contrast

The still life retains warm and cool nuances. The light field around the vessels is uneven; the paint marks continue to move even where no individual object is visible. Therefore, the background is part of the image rather than serving as a blank backdrop for the row of jugs.

This task is related to a later study of a large colour field, although the artistic result is different. In **[RED](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/colour-gives-form-looking-closely-at.html>)**, the dominant colour can immediately attract attention; here, the viewer discerns subtle transitions. The comparison reveals the artist's range of interest in colour, without reducing the restrained palette to a preparation for future vibrancy.

## "Gaze": the border hides and reveals

In "Glance," a light-coloured vertical wall partially obscures a female figure. Her face, light-coloured clothing, and outstretched arm are visible, while part of her body remains behind the architectural plane. The viewer's position becomes crucial: we see the person through a limited opening, and the boundary itself directs the image's unfolding.

The bluish field on the right contrasts with the warm, greyish stone. A light-coloured hand enters this space, becoming a small but expressive horizontal accent amid the verticals. The painting allows for various emotional readings, but the archive does not reveal the model's biography or the story of the encounter depicted. The visible tension between the revealed and the hidden is already quite meaningful.

## "Holy Stones": a dark mass in a bright field

"Holy Stones" (1989), reproduced here, are built on a different principle. Several dense, dark forms are arranged in a light environment; their surfaces are complicated by subtle markings. Unlike the light figures of "Prayer," the stones are immediately discernible. Close inspection then reveals a more subtle pattern within the overall mass.

The accessible caption doesn't provide an explanation of the symbols. Therefore, their specific meaning is not stated here. The painterly structure is important for comparison: a broad, light area enhances the weight of the dark forms, while a fine line shifts the focus of attention. The restrained palette proves capable of both drawing the image closer to the background and creating a clear contrast.

## Several paths of artistic exploration

These works demonstrate how many different problems can be solved within a limited range of colours. In "Prayer," subtle tonal differences and the relationship of a person to a large space are important. In "Jugs," variations in form and the spacing between objects are important. In "Glance," architectural boundaries and the incomplete revelation of a figure are emphasised.

Alongside the bazaar series, floral still lifes, RED, and abstractions, this series adds dimension to Valikhodjaev's creative journey. The artist varies his subjects, level of detail, and degree of contrast, continuing to explore new expressive possibilities in colour, form, and composition. A close examination reveals this continuity in the specific structure of each painting.

## A restrained palette in the artist's history

A 2012 profile in San’at highlights the lyrical, less contrasting nature of Valikhodjaev's early works. Among the works mentioned are "Prayer" and "Holy Stones." Kadysheva examines them alongside his more saturated later paintings. This approach helps to grasp the range of his artistic experience, while archival reproductions allow the viewer to test these observations in specific relationships of colour and form.

Restraint does not mean a lack of painterly effort. In "Prayer," the slight difference between the figure and its surroundings proves significant; in "Jugs," a subtle change in the edge allows for volume. For each task, the artist chooses the degree of emphasis. A later, powerful colour scheme offers a different degree of attention, but these early choices retain their own expressiveness. They offer an opportunity to slow down and notice how the image emerges from closely related tones.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. ## The early years do not resolve into a single style

The restrained palette is best considered alongside other explorations of those years. In Angel of 1993, attention rests on a seated woman, her face and hands; in Cry, painted in 1991, a dark mass and sharp light accents play a major role. These works broaden the early picture while retaining the individuality of each pictorial problem. His movement towards bazaar canvases, RED and abstraction is therefore better understood as continuing curiosity than as exchanging one definitive style for another.

## Inside the studio: before and after the exhibition

The studio reveals several stages of a pictorial decision: a canvas on the easel, a moving brush, a pause before a painted surface. Photographs from different years connect the daily work. They show how a painting’s expression emerges through observation and assessment — through the distance from which the artist studies it, and attention to the way one passage changes the whole.
