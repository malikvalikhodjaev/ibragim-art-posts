The sculpture symposium brings artists together in a shared workspace. Over the course of several weeks, works are born here: concept meets material, form is gradually refined, and the artists observe their colleagues' solutions. For the public, this is an opportunity to observe the creation of a sculpture, and for participants, it is a unique professional environment in which work and artistic exchange occur simultaneously.

Ibragim Valikhodjaev has organised and curated eight international sculpture symposia. Two were held in Turkey, in the Maltepe district of Istanbul, in 2021–2022. Six more were held in Uzbekistan: in Samarkand in 2022 and 2025, in Tashkent in 2023 and 2024, and twice in 2026. This experience forms a significant part of his work as an artist, curator, and organiser.

Valikhodjaev's interest in organising symposia coexists with his own exploration of artistic form. As a painter and sculptor, he seeks new expressive solutions; as an organiser and curator, he creates conditions for the convergence of diverse artistic approaches. Working with stone, volume, and open space expands his professional scope, extending beyond the confines of easel painting.

## The work of shaping stone

Stone sculpture develops through the gradual unfolding of form. Within the initial block, the basic masses, the direction of movement, and the ratio of volume to void must be defined. The material sets the conditions: its size, structure, and workability influence the artistic solution. The work requires envisioning the future image while simultaneously considering the specific stone.

Reports from the 2025 Samarkand symposium reveal the various stages of this work. The general outlines gradually become more defined, the surface acquires character, and individual transitions are refined. Documentation of the work process allows us to understand the many decisions contained in the completed sculpture. It is particularly noticeable how the large form emerges from many successive steps.

A shared space creates the opportunity for professional discussion. The artists work with different images and sculptural challenges, observing each other's approaches. Even without a shared style, a meaningful juxtaposition emerges: one artist emphasises mass, while another builds expression through an opening or an elongated silhouette. These differences enrich the overall artistic environment.

This international workshop allows us to see how diversely a common sculptural challenge can be approached. Mass and negative space, surface character, scale, and connection to place are each interpreted individually by each participant. This exchange reveals the exploratory nature of art: familiar materials once again open up new possibilities. Valikhodjaev, as the organiser, shapes the terms of this conversation, and each sculptor retains authorship of their work.

## Organizer and curator

Organising a symposium requires coordinating the creative program and working conditions. It's essential to coordinate participants, the venue, materials, and deadlines so that the ideas can be fully realized. The curator's task pertains to the artistic content: the overall theme, the diversity of the artist's approaches, and the relationship of the future works to the surrounding space.

Valikhodjaev combines this experience with his own practice as a painter and sculptor. A working artist knows how much a concept depends on the material and how the solution evolves during the process. This professional experience is essential for conversations with participants: it helps them discuss the artistic challenge in a concrete manner, through form, proportions, and the characteristics of the medium.

Developing projects in different cities and countries allows the organization to be viewed as a consistent line of action. Each location has its own architecture, audience, and cultural context. The program for an international meeting must take these differences into account and preserve space for the authors' autonomy. It is the combination of a common framework and individual solutions that gives the symposium its substance.

## Environmental theme "Tashkent-2024"

Official announcements of the Tashkent 2024 international symposium linked its program to ecology and climate change. This theme opens up a wide range of sculptural challenges: humans and the natural environment, water, growth, movement, and balance. Each participant can find their own image and express it through sculpture.

The meaning of the theme is revealed through a specific artistic solution. A massive volume can convey stability, an elongated form conveys movement, and an opening connects the sculpture to its surroundings. The material and scale add their own qualities. As a result, the overall idea is interpreted in different ways by the artist, which can be compared throughout the work and in the final exhibition.

The program also announced master classes for young artists and the placement of works in parks and on the grounds of Green University. This information describes the project's concept and the anticipated future life of the sculptures. When discussing a specific object, it is important to additionally consider its actual installation location and the conditions of perception.

## Sculpture after the symposium

When a work is placed in a park or urban environment, its audience changes. It is approached intentionally and encountered by chance; it is seen in sunny weather, rain, and dusk; it is approached from different angles. The work interacts with paths, greenery, buildings, and human movement. Its spatial solution continues to unfold after the work's completion.

Public sculpture must maintain its expressiveness at various distances. From a distance, the silhouette and mass relationships are visible; up close, the surface and transitions in form. These qualities are linked to decisions made on the site. Therefore, the discussion of the symposium naturally follows a discussion of the site and the viewing experience.

Valikhodjaev's eight symposia bridge the creative and public realms of art. Video footage from Maltepe and Samarkand provides a glimpse into his work, while a biography and official publications provide context. Together, they chart a long journey: from an international artistic gathering to works designed for shared spaces.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
