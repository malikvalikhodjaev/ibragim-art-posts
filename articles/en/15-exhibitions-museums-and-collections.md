But the paintings also have a longer history—museum storage, collections, new shows, and meetings with audiences. Valikhodjaev's portrait from Art Vernissage opens this conversation about the artist and the subsequent life of his works.

The artist's published biography lists public, museum, gallery, and private collections in various countries. This list allows us to trace the geography of his works and discern the diverse environments in which they continue to exist. The exhibition program complements this picture, revealing specific encounters between the works and their audiences.

## Museums: A Longer Conversation

Among the collections listed in the CV are the Yu Shun Museum in China, Harbin, and the State Museum of Arts of Uzbekistan. The museum context allows for viewing the work alongside other works, revisiting it through exhibitions, and becoming familiar with it through publications. The story of an individual artist fits into a broader conversation about painting.

In 2026, the Yu Shun Museum hosted Valikhodjaev's solo exhibition, "Sun on Snow." A video recording shows the exhibition halls, visitors, and the opening ceremony. It helps us reconstruct the circumstances of the event and appreciate the scale of these monumental works. Information about the museum collection in this article is based on the artist's published biography.

## Gallery: A New Environment for Familiar Topics

The collections include the Fine Arts Gallery of the National Bank of the Republic of Uzbekistan in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, and the Nevsky Prospect Gallery in St. Petersburg, Russia. The exhibition "From Dream to Destiny" also took place in Tashkent in 2018. An illustrated overview of the exhibition features bazaar scenes, landscapes, and other works by the artist.

The exhibition material preserves the arrangement of the works, their titles, and the impressions of the show. It is a useful complement to the reproduction of a single painting. A market theme can appear alongside a portrait or a red landscape, and such juxtaposition reveals the range of the artist's explorations. The gallery environment creates the opportunity for such juxtapositions.

## Municipal and Artists’ Association Collections

The biography mentions the collections of the Maltepe Municipality in Istanbul, Turkey, as well as the collections of the Union of Artists of Azerbaijan and the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. The list also includes the National Committee on Ecology and Climate Change of the Republic of Uzbekistan. These institutions provide different social contexts for the presence of art. The artist's works are also held in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.

This geographic reach reflects the breadth of Valikhodjaev's professional work: he works as a painter, sculptor, curator, and organiser of international projects. He has organised and curated eight international sculpture symposia in Turkey and Uzbekistan. Each role has its own context: the artist creates the work, while the organiser helps establish the conditions for the overall artistic process.

## Private Collections: Living with a Painting

According to his CV, Valikhodjaev's works are held in private collections in the United States, Canada, Germany, Italy, France, Russia, the Philippines, Korea, China, and other countries. A private collection has its own way of encountering art: a work can become part of the owner's everyday environment and gradually reveal itself upon repeated viewing.

A recognisable human scene allows one to enter the painting through a gesture or familiar action. An architectural landscape captures attention with the relationships between planes and roads. An abstract work offers an independent discussion of colour and form. The diversity of these objectives helps us understand why the artist's work can be viewed in such diverse environments.

## Preserving a Work’s Connection to the Artist

Accurate information about a work's title, artist, material, and history supports its future life. Documents and images that allow for recognition in new publications are also important alongside the painting. In this blog, archival reproductions are accompanied by titles, and details are linked to the complete images.

A biography, exhibition notes, and photographs of his works help connect the various facets of Valikhodjaev's practice. In his "Bazaar" series, he was interested in people and everyday life; in "RED," in the possibilities of the colour red; in "New View," in abstraction. Museums and collections preserve the results of this exploration, and new viewers continue it with their attention to his works.

## Exhibition and collection: different pages of history

The history of a public exhibition and the history of a work's ownership may overlap, but require different information. The opening report confirms the artist's participation and the venue; the collection entry indicates where the work is held. For example, UzA's announcement of a group exhibition in Beijing on January 23, 2024, lists Valikhodjaev among the artists featured. This adds to his exhibition history, but does not in itself confirm the painting's entry into the venue's collection.

For the reader, this distinction adds depth to the biography. One canvas may appear in several temporary exhibitions, then return to its owner; another remains in the museum collection and is given a new lease of life in subsequent shows. When getting to know an artist, it's helpful to read the caption, exhibition source, and collection information together. Then the work acquires a concrete history, and international geography becomes more than just a list of countries.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
