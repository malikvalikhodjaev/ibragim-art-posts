# Ibragim Valikhodjaev — Biography, Exhibitions and Artist CV

[https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>)

**Ibragim Valikhodjaev** (Ибрагим Валиходжаев) is a professional Uzbek painter and sculptor, art expert, curator and organiser of international art projects based in Uzbekistan, Tashkent. He is Chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan and an Academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan.

Born in 1958, he explores contemporary life, memory and Uzbek culture through a wide range of genres and series: Namangan bazaar scenes and everyday life; RED; early monochrome and restrained-palette paintings; nude figure studies, portraits and still lifes; village landscapes and plein air paintings of Uzbekistan, including Miraki; Montenegro landscapes; monumental abstraction; and metal sculpture, including floating works. His continuing research into colour, form, rhythm and composition connects these different directions. His colour ranges from clearly defined, contrasting planes to subtle transitions between warm and cool tones.

He combines studio practice with the organisation and curation of exhibitions and international sculpture symposia. He has organised and curated eight symposia in Turkey and Uzbekistan and participated in plein air projects in both countries. His international work and creative visits also include China, Azerbaijan, Cyprus, Georgia and Kazakhstan.

As an art expert, he founded and chairs the Expert Commission for the Evaluation of Artworks in Uzbekistan and has developed valuation rates for artistic works.

*Alternative transliterations of his name include Ibrahim Valikhodjaev and Ibrokhim Valikhodjaev.*

## Institutional roles and teaching

- **2026–present:** Lecturer in the master's programme, Kimyo International University in Tashkent.
- **2023–present:** Academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan.
- **2017–present:** Chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan.
- **2017–present:** Founder and Chairman of the Expert Commission for the Evaluation of Artworks in the Republic of Uzbekistan; developed valuation rates for artistic works.
- **Until 2026:** Head of Artistic Works and member of the Expert Council during the construction of the Center of Islamic Civilization in Uzbekistan, Tashkent.
- **1999–2012:** Chairman of the Art Fund of the Republic of Uzbekistan.
- **1997–1999:** Chairman, Namangan branch of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan.
- **1997–1999:** Chairman, Namangan branch of the Art Fund of Uzbekistan.

## Education and gallery founding

**1986–1991:** Studio of Master Rahim Ahmedov; Faculty of Painting, Alexander Nikolayevich Ostrovsky Institute. See the master's painting [«For Whom» at Sotheby's](<https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2014/at-the-crossroads-2-l14009/lot.13.html>).

**2021–2026:** Founder, Valikhodjaev Gallery House, Uzbekistan, Tashkent; opened 5 August 2021.

**1993–1999:** Founder, «Tumor» Gallery Salon, Uzbekistan, Namangan; the first private art gallery in Uzbekistan.

His work includes the organisation and leadership of artistic works for government buildings, cultural institutions, transport infrastructure and public monuments.

- **Senate of Uzbekistan (Upper House of Parliament), Uzbekistan, Tashkent:** Organised and led the artistic works for the Senate building.

- **Center of Islamic Civilization, Uzbekistan, Tashkent:** Head of Artistic Works throughout the construction of the Center; directed and organised the artistic works and served on its Expert Council. Work continued until 2026.
- **Tashkent International Airport, Terminal 2, Uzbekistan, Tashkent:** Organised and led the artistic works for the international airport terminal.
- **«Friendship of Peoples» metro station, Uzbekistan, Tashkent:** Organised and led artistic works for the metro station.
- **Memorial at the mass graves and other public monuments, Uzbekistan:** Artistic work on the memorial at the mass graves and other monuments in public spaces.
- **Other state and cultural commissions, Uzbekistan:** Artistic direction for government premises, parks, palaces of culture and large public complexes; organisation and coordination of artistic works on state projects.

## Additional teaching and institutional experience

Archival records also document his work as a tutor at the Mannon Uygur Tashkent State Art Institute (1991–1994), Chairman of the Special Fund of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan (1999–2005), teacher at the Kamoliddin Bekhzod National Institute of Arts and Design (2006–2007), and Head of Creative Activities and Exhibition Coordination at the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan (2017–2018). The relationship with Master Rahim Ahmedov illuminates this continuity. Training in his studio in 1986–1991 provided Valikhodjaev with a professional foundation; his own language developed through sustained work from life, figure compositions and colour. The exhibition photograph with his teacher adds a human encounter to the biographical record. It does not reduce the pupil’s paintings to an extension of the master’s manner: training offers a basis for independent discovery.

His career developed alongside changes in Uzbekistan’s art institutions. The Academy of Arts was established on 23 January 1997; that year Valikhodjaev became chairman of the Namangan branch of the Union of Artists. He later headed the Academy’s Special Fund in 1999–2005 and the Art Fund of Uzbekistan in 1999–2012. His Union chairmanship since 2017, appointment as an Academician in 2023, gallery work and international projects show how making art can connect with creating conditions for others. This is a parallel between a biography and its cultural setting, rather than a claim that one artist determined that setting’s development.

## Plein air begins with looking

Plein air begins well before the first brushstroke, with a walk and a choice of viewpoint. These photographs show a village lane, an enclosed courtyard, a rocky slope and autumn trees. One motif can be read as an alternation of pale walls and dark openings; another as a road moving between buildings. The painter chooses both what to depict and the arrangement of broad masses that will hold the future picture together.

These working photographs complement the landscapes by restoring a sense of scale, uneven light and surface texture. Walls, roofs, a strip of earth and a tree crown supply material for colour relationships. In the finished painting, observation may become freer and more condensed; its connection with the motif remains in the organisation of space. The photographs have no established location or year: their appearance alone cannot assign them to a particular plein-air programme in Miraki, Montenegro or Azerbaijan.

## RED: painting before an audience

Two photographs of the masterclass accompanying RED in Tashkent in 2017 bring the discussion of colour into practice. Visitors gather close to the easel and follow the sequence of actions. Speed is only one part of the demonstration: the silhouette, the extent of a colour patch and the intervals between forms become visible decisions.

The Embassy of Uzbekistan’s report on the exhibition records 31 works and a masterclass for visitors. The photographs open the process to view: the position of the hand, the palette and the emerging image can be compared. The investigation of red takes shape through its neighbouring tones and the organisation of the entire canvas.

## Inside the studio: before and after the exhibition

The studio reveals several stages of a pictorial decision: a canvas on the easel, a moving brush, a pause before a painted surface. Photographs from different years connect the daily work. They show how a painting’s expression emerges through observation and assessment — through the distance from which the artist studies it, and attention to the way one passage changes the whole.

## The painting and its exhibition space

Installation photographs reveal another part of a painting’s life: its encounter with viewers. Empty wall space, the intervals between pictures and room to step back alter the conditions of looking. The wider view establishes scale; the photograph with visitors shows the habit of approaching a work and returning to it from a distance.
