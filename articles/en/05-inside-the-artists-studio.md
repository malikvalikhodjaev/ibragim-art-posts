A finished painting conceals the many decisions that led to its creation. In the studio, this process is visible: the artist compares colour relationships, refines the silhouette, changes the size of the spot, and steps back from the canvas to re-examine the whole. Painting reveals itself as a careful work of observation and form, in which each new brushstroke depends on the one already painted.

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's creative practice is intertwined with teaching. Archival footage from August 8, 2014, shows him working in a studio with and for students. Video footage from Azerbaijan from 2026 captures the open process of painting during plein air. Both formats offer the opportunity to witness a professional solution emerge directly before the viewer.

The studio allows us to see how artistic curiosity translates into practical work. Valikhodjaev constantly seeks new approaches to colour, form, and composition: in the Namangan series, he was interested in the people and life of the bazaar; in RED, the possibilities of red; and in New View, abstract language. Each direction requires its own experiments, observations, and refinements on the canvas.

## The studio of Master Rahim Ahmedov

An important stage in Valikhodjaev's education is connected to the workshop of Master Rahim Ahmedov: from 1986 to 1991, he studied at the Alexander Ostrovsky Tashkent Theatre and Art Institute. In the artist's biography, this connection marks a specific artistic school and a period of professional development. Working alongside the master allows him to master painting through his own practice and analysis of his work.

Art school imparts ways of seeing and comparing. In drawing, this means proportions and the character of form; in painting, tonal and colour relationships; in composition, the connection of parts to the whole. These skills gradually become the foundation of independent work. The artist's individual language develops as they explore their own themes, drawing on their acquired experience.

Rahim Ahmedov's work can also be found in the Sotheby's catalog, which records his painting ‘For Whom’. This link expands our knowledge of Valikhodjaev's artistic circle and allows us to explore the work of his teacher. To understand their continuity, it's helpful to study both artists while maintaining an awareness of the independence of their artistic approaches.

## How observation becomes an image

The beginning of a work involves selection. There are too many details in the surrounding world for them all to be given equal space on the canvas. The artist determines which relationships are important: a large, light volume against a dark background, the rotation of a figure, the spatial line, a characteristic colour. From these observations, a pictorial concept gradually emerges.

Then you have to reconcile the particular with the general. A well-chosen detail can be too noticeable; a striking colour can disrupt the relationships of adjacent areas. Therefore, the work develops through constant comparison. Stepping back from the canvas allows you to see the composition as a whole, while moving closer allows you to refine a particular transition, edge, or brushstroke. Changing the distance becomes a working tool.

Watching the process on video, one can focus on the sequence of actions. Where does the artist pause? Which part does he return to? How does a new section transform its neighbours? Even a brief scene demonstrates that the brushstroke exists within a system of relationships. Its size, direction, and colour acquire meaning across the entire surface of the painting.

## Sharing experience with students

Openly working in front of students makes professional choices visible. They can hear an explanation and immediately see the change it produces on the canvas. In such a lesson, the connection between words and actions is crucial: a discussion of proportion, tone, or composition is concretely confirmed in the image. It's easier for students to understand the intended outcome of the proposed technique.

No less important is the skill of independent comparison. The student gradually learns to see why one area stands out too much, where the form loses its integrity, how the colour patchs support each other. This requires repeated work and careful analysis. Confidence develops as one's own observation becomes more precise.

Valikhodjaev's published biography also lists his teaching assignment at the master's program at Kimyo International University in Tashkent, beginning in 2026. Archival studies, open demonstrations, and university work represent various aspects of his teaching practice. They are united by the transmission of experience through works of art, process, and professional discourse.

For a student, the process of exploration itself is particularly meaningful. The artist compares colour relationships, tests the strength of a colour patch, refines the silhouette, and returns to the overall structure of the painting. Attention to these decisions helps understand professional work as the development of a concept. The transfer of experience in the studio is also linked to a willingness to ask new questions, try a different approach, and notice how a small change transforms the whole.

## Studio and plein air

In the studio, the artist can return to the image for longer periods and work in relatively stable conditions. Outdoors, the light and surrounding environment change more rapidly. The ability to identify key relationships and maintain them throughout the work becomes especially important. These conditions develop observation and concentration in different ways.

In the 2026 Azerbaijani recording, the process is open to people standing nearby. Viewers see the surface change as the work progresses, and can compare individual actions with the final result. This format helps to perceive painting as a professional endeavor, requiring attention, experience, and consistent choice.

A tour of the studio enriches the subsequent perception of the finished paintings. The colour scheme reveals a distinct solution, the confident silhouette reveals the refinement of form, and the composition reveals the result of numerous comparisons. The exhibition displays the completed work, and video records of the process help us see how it develops within the artist's practice.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
