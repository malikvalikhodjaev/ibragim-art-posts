"Pink Abstraction No. 1" offers a different way of thinking about composition. Rhythm is formed by the brushstrokes, the density of the paint field, and the position of a single expressive line.

## What happens within a colour field

At first glance, "Pink Abstraction No. 1" appears as a broad surface of a single dominant colour. Upon closer inspection, it reveals a multitude of variations: the pink approaches red, lightens in places, and then solidifies again. The absence of a recognisable object heightens the significance of these subtle changes. They now form the content of the visual experience.

The title sets the colour guide, but it doesn't exhaust the painting. Within the pink field, there are directions, boundaries, and varying densities of brushstrokes. They determine where the eye will linger and how it will move to the next area. The viewer is presented not with an empty, painted plane, but with a surface organised by the work of painting.

## The horizontal changes the balance

Below the centre runs a dark horizontal stripe. It thickens and thins in places, maintaining its unevenness and slight variations. This line divides the field, but does not make the two parts identical. The top occupies a larger area; the bottom feels more collected due to the proximity of the line and the edge of the canvas.

The horizontal becomes the reference point against which the movement of the brushstrokes can be felt. The vertical traces from above approach it, while fine horizontal strokes support its direction. The meeting of these two rhythms imbues this seemingly simple solution with an internal tension. This effect doesn't require a multitude of contrasting colours: a precise relationship between the line and the large field is sufficient.

## Intersections and intermittent patterns

In the upper part, intersecting vertical and horizontal traces are discernible. They resemble a loose grid: in some places, the repetition is clearer, in others, it dissolves into colour. The eye recognises the direction and simultaneously notices its disruption. Therefore, the surface maintains a rhythm without becoming mechanically regular.

It's useful to compare these areas with the looser brushstrokes below. The difference in density creates pauses between the richer sections. You can begin with a dark stripe, move to subtle intersections, and then return to a nearly open colour. With each transition, the focus shifts—from the overall composition to the structure of the paint layer.

## How to look at colour up close

Upon closer inspection, the brushstroke itself becomes important: its width, direction, and degree of transparency. Some brushstrokes almost blend into the overall field, while others leave a more distinct border. These subtle differences determine whether the surface appears calm or internally dynamic.

Even in a general view, one can see how one pink shade changes next to another. A light trace appears more noticeable next to a dense red, a thin dark line gains weight thanks to a large coloured surround. Viewed this way, colour ceases to be a conventional symbol of mood and reveals itself as a system of relationships.

## Rhythm without repeating figures

In Valikhodjaev's bazaar paintings, rhythm can be traced in the scarves, the silhouettes of people, and the curves of hands. In this abstraction, the role of repetition is played by the direction of the brushstrokes and the alternation of dense and sparse areas. This juxtaposition helps us understand the connections between the different directions of his work, while maintaining the distinctions between them.

"**[New View](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post_06.html>)**" is connected to the artist's interest in abstraction, "RED" to the exploration of red, and the Namangan Series to the people and everyday life of the bazaar. "Pink Abstraction No. 1" allows us to examine the very concept of rhythm through a distinct colour field. ## General view and slow reading

Try first taking in the entire painting and determining the position of the dark horizontal line. Then trace a few subtle vertical traces and compare them to the lower portion. This simple transition helps you see how the overall structure depends on subtle differences within colour.

Abstraction leaves room for personal perception, but the basis for conversation is quite concrete: the size of the field, the direction of the line, the distribution of brushstrokes, the place of pause. These relationships reveal the artist's constant curiosity for new expressive solutions. Rhythm here arises in the very process of organising the pictorial surface.

## One colour, several sensations of space

The position of the dark band allows us to test how little is needed to transform the whole. Looking primarily at the top, the field appears open and expansive. If we pause at the bottom edge, the proximity of the horizontal makes this area appear more confined and dense. The hues remain closely related, but the sense of space changes. The line becomes the boundary between two ways of perceiving the same surface.

Particularly expressive are the places where the thin vertical trace meets the stripe. Here, different kinds of brush movement meet: a long stroke and a short mark, a nearly even direction and a slight deviation. The paint trace preserves these differences on the motionless canvas. For the viewer, they become a reason to move from the overall colour impression to a particular gesture and then back to the whole.

In this respect, the pink work differs from the geometric compositions of the "New View," where clear contours separate several colours. Here, the boundary exists within a closely related colour range, and the surface is active through the shades and application of paint. This juxtaposition reveals the breadth of Valikhodjaev's abstract exploration: clearly defined planes and freer brushwork offer different ways to construct an expressive image.

## About the artist

Ibragim Valikhodjaev is an artist and sculptor from Uzbekistan, an academician of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan, and the chairman of the Union of Artists of Uzbekistan. He also works as a curator, expert, teacher, and organiser of international art projects; he founded the Valikhodjaev Gallery House. [Biography, exhibitions, and CV](<https://ibragim-valikhodjaev.blogspot.com/2026/10/blog-post.html>) .

Ibragim Valikhodjaev's works are held in the Yu Shun Art Museum (China, Harbin), in the collection of the Maltepe Municipality (Turkey, Istanbul), and also in a private collection in Moscow, Russia.
